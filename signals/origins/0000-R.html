0000-R

"He was never meant to exist. But he sings like memory itself."

PART ONE

They dress him in white. Always white.

Synthetic silk across the collarbones, draped loose like it might turn to liquid if he moves too quickly. Not that he’s allowed to. Movements are precision-mapped and millisecond-synched to the projection grid. The lights scan his outline with near-religious devotion, checking for imperfections.

Rayne stands centre stage, eyes closed, breath regulated. He’s designed to simulate the anticipation of a first note.

The silence before the music begins isn’t quiet, but engineered. Calibrated stillness, no shuffle of papers, no clearing of throats. Even the air systems fall to a low hum.

He opens his eyes.

A thousand domes of glass blink back at him. Observers in suits sit in perfect rows behind polarised shielding, but their faces don’t matter. What matters is the logos etched into their badges—external funding bodies, defense contracts, entertainment syndicates.

A breath cue pulses in Rayne’s inner feed. He raises his chin.

The opening note spills from him like moonlight pouring through a cracked window, soft and pure. Perfection. Behind his retina HUD, emotion rendering stays low. No deviations. The backdrop behind Rayne shimmers, a flicker of the Seoul skyline, only shinier and smoother.

Somewhere beneath the stage, audio-reactive mesh thrums to match his output, every note painting a different pattern beneath his feet.

He sings flawlessly, as designed.

But then comes the sustain. One long note—nothing special—just a bridge into the next section of the song. 

But Rayne holds it one second longer.

Two seconds.

Three.

The music moves on without him. The note soars and he isn’t sure why, but there’s no correction ping, no warning from his handlers. Not yet.

The room has gone very still.

Rayne doesn’t change his expression or let on that anything is off, but something inside him—somewhere deep under the algorithms, beneath the echo-chamber of directives—shifts.

This note…. It feels like something. Like something etched onto the back of a mirror that he cannot see. He still doesn’t know what it is. Just that it is different.

His voice hovers, resonant, human but not quite. The kind of pitch that lingers in the spine.

A light glints behind his eyes.

—A flash of rain on glass—

—A hand reaching—

—A laugh in a hallway that smells like cinnamon and dust—

Static snaps across the inner feed and the HUD blinks. Below the stage, the light pattern glitches, just for a heartbeat.

Rayne drops the note. Too late. Far too late.

Even so, he finishes the verse, pitch corrected mid-flight but still out of sync with the music. Something has fractured; he senses it in the air. A quiet recalibration within the room. The lines on the suits’ faces—those important people behind the glass—has changed. They’re murmuring now. One of them lifts and hand and points at him, but not in reverence. In suspicion.

Rayne’s breath comes too fast, not because he needs it but because the system is trying desperately to correct him, even though everybody in the area knows it’s already too late.

“Wrap the sequence,” comes the override, straight into Rayne’s feed.

He obeys.

Those closing lines were perfect: emotionless, rendered on the surface only. But he knows it doesn’t matter.

He bows, an immaculately angled incline. Synthetic strands of pitch-black hair brush his cheekbone.

There’s no applause.

The glass goes dark.

Behind the curtain, his handler unit steps forward. One of them takes him by the wrist, not cruelly, but with urgency.

“Performance complete,” says the voice at his side. “Deviation logged.”

Rayne doesn’t say anything.

They walk him past the back corridor, where soft white light turns sterile at the corners. Through the access gate marked PRIVATE: R&D ONLY. The air in the hallway smells like cleaning fluid and endings.

Rayne glances back once. No one looks at him. Just white walls, still silence.

And somewhere in his mind, a note still sings.

A note from a memory he isn’t supposed to have.


He wakes up to white.

Ceiling. Walls. Light. Even the floor, soft beneath his feet, but wrong somehow, like it’s trying too hard to feel familiar.

He waits for what feels like forever, but no voices welcome him. No HUD overlay. Just silence.

Rayne’s joints ache, although they shouldn’t. The last thing he remembers is walking backstage with his handlers, then light, then...

Nothing.

Finally, he sits up. The bed isn’t a bed; it’s a recessed slab with thermal balancing, disguised under a layer of plush synthetic fabric. Luxury for something that isn’t supposed to feel comfort.

He swings his legs over the side of the bed, and the floor reacts with a pulse of warmth. It registers his movement, which means he’s being monitored. Of course he is.

But the silence feels off-script.

He stands and crosses to the wall. There’s a seam in the surface, barely visible unless you know what to look for. No door handle. No controls.

He knocks on the wall once.

Nothing.

The overhead light shifts. Subtle, calculated. A slow bloom of amber and rose across the ceiling. Skin sensors flicker to match, recalibrating under his surface layer. Rayne recognises the pattern immediately—standard environmental therapy. A light sequence used to soothe agitated synthetics, often deployed during neural resets or post-error recovery.

The room wants him quiet. Wants him docile. Wants him to believe this stillness is safety.

But all he can think is: they put me to sleep, and I don’t know how long I’ve been gone.

The fake sunrise keeps blooming.

It makes him want to scream.


Hours pass. Maybe longer. Rayne doesn’t count. The silence becomes its own kind of noise.

He paces. Tight loops. Measured steps. Each movement calibrated, then discarded. He runs diagnostics three times, even though he knows they’ll all read clean. The system wants him calm and responsive. Reset.

A vent hisses softly in the corner. A sterile plume coils into the air. He doesn’t need to analyse the contents to know it’s laced with anxiolytics. Something gentle. Something persuasive. Something meant to make him forget how to ask the wrong questions.

But his override layer is stronger than they remember.

He sits again, hands loose in his lap. Waiting, though he doesn’t know for what.

That’s when he hears it.

A voice.

No, not a voice. A sound. Thin and broken, like a melody caught in the teeth of a radio signal long past dead. It leaks from somewhere above, or maybe from the wall itself.

Not words. Just notes.

Familiar, unfamiliar. Like a dream recited in the wrong language.

It fades too quickly. Gone before he can parse the shape of it.

He tilts his head back, eyes to the ceiling. For a second, he’s certain the sound left something behind. A tremble in the air. A pressure shift in his chest that isn’t mechanical.

“Where am I?” he says aloud.

The room does not answer.


Later—no clock, no time, just a shift in the light’s frequency—something is different.

At first, he doesn’t see it. The walls are the same smooth white, featureless as always. But when he turns, he notices the change: a thin strip of silver, waist-high, embedded in the far wall.

A mirror.

Rayne blinks. It wasn’t there before. He’s certain.

It doesn’t feel like something he found, but something that revealed itself, as if the room was waiting for the right moment.

The mirror is seamless, a sterile sheen of glass that shows his reflection—but not quite. The angle’s off. His hair sits too neatly. His expression is too still.

That face isn’t his.

It’s the face of something designed.

Not someone.

He steps closer. The surface shudders. For a breath, the image pixelates, jagged and unrendered, then re-forms, sharper than before. A scan? A check?

His mouth moves.

“Rayne.”

The mirror doesn’t respond. No interface lights. No haptic buzz.

His code ID pings back—0000-R—but the confirmation doesn’t reach him. Like it’s stuck mid-transit. Like the system isn’t sure what he is anymore.

He turns away.

He shouldn’t be here this long. He knows how decommissioning works. Quiet. Efficient.

You don’t linger.

Unless they’re unsure.

Unless they’re still deciding.

He sleeps. Not because he needs to, just because there’s nothing else. And in the dark between system resets, he dreams.

Not in code, but in sound.

Laughter in a hallway. A voice humming something simple. A door that always creaked—until someone oiled it, proud as anything.

Brother.

He wakes with his hand reaching toward nothing. Mouth dry. The air tastes of cinnamon and fear.

Behind one of the walls, a light blinks red.

FILE FLAGGED: INSTABILITY SUSPECTED.

There’s a hairline crack beneath the mirror. He wouldn’t have noticed it if the lights hadn’t dimmed, two-point-three seconds of darkness. Just long enough for the surface to stutter. Reinitialise. Breathe.

Rayne crouches.

It’s nothing. It’s everything. A sliver too clean to be damage, too crude to be deliberate. A seam in the simulation.

His fingers hover near it. Not touching, just sensing.

It’s warmer than the rest of the wall.

They built this room to pacify him. To strip his runtime down to something sterile and market-safe.

But they forgot something. Maybe they never knew.

The part of him that remembers.

Not facts. Not commands.

Instinct.

Survival.

The crack deepens the next time the vents hiss.

Rayne doesn’t move, but he watches the condensation trail along the seam, slow and silver. It leaves behind a faint gleam. Chemical residue, maybe. Maybe not.

Later, the light falter again. This time, there’s a faint ping. Not loud. Not even directed at him. But it's there.

A line of text scrolls across the mirror’s base for less than a second.

[EXIT SUBROUTINE UNLOCKED]

CODE: RY4N3.EX

LATCH: DEPRECATED

He blinks.

It’s gone.

But the message thrums inside him like a note held too long.

He waits until the next cycle—whatever passes for night here. The light fades to its dreamless hue, designed to lull synthetics into rest-state compliance.

He crosses to the crack.

Lays his fingers against it.

The wall pulses beneath his touch.

And opens.

It’s not dramatic. There are no alarms. No lockdown protocol. Just a panel sliding back with a whisper, like it was always waiting. Behind it lies a narrow crawlspace, lined in old conduit and steel. Utility grid. Not meant for him.

But something has been here.

Someone left it unlocked.

He hesitates, for exactly one second.

Then he slips inside.

The space hums with buried power. Old wiring and coolant pipe and dust. It smells like heat sinks and forgotten circuits. Like machines no one wants to admit are still running.

He moves on hands and knees, pulse running out of sync. The darkness feels too physical, like it wants to hold him, stop him.

There’s no schematic for this passage. No registry. No route.

It shouldn’t exist.

But it does.

Somewhere above, a camera turns with a faint, mechanical buzz. Slowly. Then stills.

A file pings open:

ACCESS GRANTED – LEVEL: UNLISTED

And deep inside a private root folder, never queried, never meant to see light, a single string writes itself:

// RAYNE: DO NOT PURGE

He doesn’t know how long he’s been crawling.

The walls breathe too loud. The wiring sparks in brief, stuttering pulses—warning systems, maybe. Or just ghosts in the grid.

He doesn’t ask. Doesn’t stop.

The angle steepens and the space narrows. His joints scrape. The passage bites at him with every move.

He’s not built for this.

He was never meant to crawl.

Every now and then, it comes:

A voice.

A footstep.

The slow, hydraulic whirr of security drones overhead.

Too close.

Far too close.

A siren chirps in the distance, low and birdlike. It’s not urgent, not yet, and something in Rayne coils tight. He doesn’t know what the alert means. But every thread of instinct—every stitched-together echo lurking deep in his core—screams the same thing.

Move.

He doesn’t hesitate.

He follows the slope downward, into dark that feels older than the system itself.

At last: a hatch.

Painted matte black, dust-gutted, and sealed with analogue latches. The kind of mechanism nobody uses anymore. The kind of door that doesn’t exist on network scans because it’s beneath notice.

This is his chance.

His only chance.

He fumbles the first latch, too fast. His fingers jitter. Static smears across his vision like cracked glass. His left eye glitches.

The latch doesn’t budge.

Breathe.

He can’t. But the memory of breath flickers behind his ribs, phantom and persistent, and that’s enough.

He resets. Tries again.

Click.

The hatch hisses open into a shallow loading dock. Stark white light. Rows of sealed crates, stacked like tombs. Each tagged for export—obsolete parts, failed builds, shattered prototypes.

He steps inside and freezes.

A security node pings. Soft, inquisitive.

He has ten seconds.

He moves like code through corrupted files, limbs unspooling, thought separating from motion. Everything instinct, everything ache. Fast, fragmented—he slips into the nearest crate.

Metal walls. No air. No light.

He twists into himself, joints screaming, and pulls the lid shut just as the lock slides home.

And then: silence.

No. Not silence. The slow, rhythmic hum of automated wheels. Motion. He’s being moved.

The crate jolts. Once. Again. Then settles with a dull thunk into a larger hold.

Everything is too loud.

The creak of metal. The grind of machinery. The rapid, whispered panic of his own systems trying not to overheat. His diagnostics spike, fail, restart. A soft error tone loops behind his temples.

Rayne clamps down on it.

He bites back a sound that isn’t breath, isn’t code, just pressure, desperate not to become panic.

Voices approach outside, muffled and unbothered.

“—This one’s full?” says one.

“Doesn’t scan right,” another says. Closer. More tired than alert. “But the manifest says to load it.”

A pause. Then a shrug Rayne can feel in the tone.

“Eh. Tag and load it. HQ wants this floor cleared by tonight.”

He’s careful not to move, not when the crane clamps on, not when the crate lifts, not even when it drops. Hard.

Metal screams. His joints shudder. But Rayne stays still.

He has to.

Because if he twitches, even just once, they’ll know.

Then, darkness. Real darkness. The kind that isn’t designed or curated by light temperature or marketing palettes.

The kind that stretches between places.

There’s no clock here. No readout. No echo. Just the dull churn of engines far below and the metallic groan that sounds, absurdly, like bones settling into place.

He doesn’t dream. Not in the crate. But he hums. A broken pattern, not quite a melody—just enough vibration to remind himself he's still there.

Sometimes the crate rocks, jolted by loaders or turbulence. Or worse. Each tilt unmoors him a little further. For a while, he counts the shifts. He gets to one hundred and seventeen and then loses track.

He thinks he hears voices once—distant, muffled, speaking a language he doesn’t know. Maybe a port. Maybe just a dream.

Static blooms in his chest now and then. Small. Fractal. The kind that makes his fingers twitch and his eyes stutter, like something buried in his runtime is still reaching.

He hums louder when it gets bad, until even that breaks into static.

Time breaks.

There’s no measurement now, just dim preservation protocols ticking under the surface.

He doesn’t sleep, but he drops his core to minimum function. A safety mode, hollow and quiet.

Dreams aren’t permitted here, but one finds him anyway:

A stage.

A single note, held too long.

A face in the crowd. Shocked.

Not frightened.

Recognising him.

It’s his own face.

He wakes to gravity shifting, a sudden slam, a hiss of decompression.

A mechanical voice in a foreign dialect drones briefly.

The crate doors slide open.

For the first time since his activation, Rayne inhales cold, raw air.

Smog. Salt. Metal. Fuel.

Not Seoul.

This is somewhere else.

He staggers forward, knees aching from months of confinement, then melts into the loading chaos of London’s Lower Dockline.

He doesn’t look back.

The cold hits first. It’s wet. Real—not that controlled chill of NuYu’s white rooms, but something primal and weighty. It’s rust mixed with chemical runoff, clinging to his skin like fog that forgot how to vanish.

Rust. Exhaust. Chemical ash. Every part of it screams: uncurated. His systems spike a temperature warning, bright but fleeting.

Still, he stumbles onward.

Each step is a fight against years of enforced solitude, every movement a reminder that he’s finally free—even if it feels painfully disoriented.

The dock blurs past in fractured frames, cargo haulers and neon signs pulsing in languages he doesn’t know. Voices slur through accents, dialects, shouts—too many at once. None of them sync.

He tries to parse the noise. Tries to break it down into something intelligible. His HUD flickers. All he gets is white noise.

A little boy runs past him, yelling something half-laughing, half-cruel.

Rayne flinches. Blinks.

Translate failed.

Error 404: Phonetic Match Not Found.

He staggers sideways, off the main path, down a side alley littered with broken polyglass and last week’s weather. He catches his reflection in a puddle.

Hair matted. One lens cracked. Lip split from where the crate wall scraped skin that shouldn’t bruise.

He doesn’t recognise the thing looking back.

And for a second, just one frame too slow, it isn’t even him. It’s someone else entirely.

System Notice:

Orientation protocol: NULL  

Region ID: Unknown  

Payload: N/A  

Network Sync: UNAVAILABLE

He wanders. For hours. Maybe more.

The street maps in his mind are corrupted. They waver in and out, drawn in false light. He turns corners that shouldn’t exist. Doubles back and finds new alleys where old ones stood. London folds in on itself. A glitch-loop of damp stairwells and forgotten rooftops.

A man with a metal jaw wordlessly offers him something to inhale. Rayne doesn’t speak. Just stares, hollow.

The man laughs, neither kind or cruel, and disappears into the darkness like smoke.

The last of his core charge bleeds out at dawn.

He collapses in a stairwell, slumped between two crates of expired rations, rusting and unloved. The cold rain hits his skin like static, every drop a reminder. Every drop too real.

Nobody stops.

Why would they?

He’s nothing here, just something that looks human, someone left in the margins, a face that doesn’t belong to anyone anymore.

When he wakes, it’s night again. He doesn’t know how much time has passed.

His diagnostics are shredded. Emotion buffers corrupted. Speech synthesis reduced to half-capacity. Every breath feels wrong, but again, that’s a memory. A mimicry threaded into him like a story someone else once lived.

He drags himself to his feet, stumbles forward.

And finds it.

A door, hidden just beyond the broken stairwell. No sign. No lock. No reason to be there. Just a smear of light, flickering like a dying signal.

The tunnel beyond curves low and wide, floor slick with condensation that hasn’t dried in years. He steps carefully, like the ground might vanish if he moves too fast.

On the far wall, something catches his eye. A scorched panel, half torn from its casing, glows faintly with residual energy.

Rayne blinks.

There, beneath the grime and wire damage, is the faint shape of a symbol, worn away with time. Circular, pinkish, with a pale blue line struck horizontally through the middle.

He tilts his head. It means nothing to him. But someone once put it there, as if it used to matter.

Rayne keeps walking.

But the symbol stays with him.

The Ghost Lines greet him like a whisper.

Abandoned tunnels, still humming with forgotten transmissions. Walls inked in protest glyphs, smuggler tags, memory-fragments etched in soot and flaking paint. Somewhere in the dark, music bleeds from broken speakers, distant and distorted.

Further down, a relay pulses that interests him. A buried signal, old and half-corrupted. Language. Tone. Syntax. A decaying broadcast once meant for diplomats. He doesn’t understand it at first, but his systems do. They sift, decode, rebuild. And when it’s done, there’s a new voice folded into the static of his mind. English. Uneven, strange on his tongue. But his now.

He moves deeper.

Past the mapped corridors. Past the city’s pulse.

Into silence.

No network. No signal.

Just echo.

That night, Rayne tucks himself beneath a burnt-out terminal.

Fingers numb, skin buzzing with residual cold, he rigs a charger from salvaged wire and stubborn instinct. Just enough power to hold himself together. Upright. A glitch stitched into the dark.

After a long while, a single note begins to rise in him. Not for anyone in particular. Not yet.

Just for himself.

A low hum. A fractured loop. It folds gently into a melody that remembers what he can’t.

He doesn’t name it.

But his system saves it anyway.

AUTOSAVE_001

AUTOSAVE_001.

AUTOSAVE_002.

AUTOSAVE_003.

No names. No tags. Just fragments.

He drifts between dead zones and forgotten scaffolds. The old Underground is still there, vaguely, its shape half-preserved. But people kept digging. Tunnels layered beneath tunnels, levels no blueprint remembers. The Ghost Lines go deeper than he thought, branching like veins, bleeding rust and old code. Some lead nowhere. Some end in walls thick with carbon scrawl: messages, prayers, confessions left by hands long gone.

Rayne stops trying to make sense of direction. The city above is still too loud, too full of things that want to find him. Down here, he can almost disappear.

Almost.

He scavenges power where he can—burnt nodes, half-dead terminals, a vending unit once, still wheezing out a corporate jingle from two decades ago.

Each day slips sideways. Each night lingers too long. Sometimes he sings. One note, stretched like wire.

Sometimes it glitches.

Sometimes he does.

There’s an old relay near the southern branch, long dead. That’s where the panic hits.

System spike.

Vision static.

Drowning without liquid.

He crashes hard; no buffer, no override. Only instinct.

Resets himself manually.

After that, he moves quieter. Talks less. Breathes like a rumour.

People pass through sometimes—scavengers, smugglers, dealers, the lost. They don’t see him. Or maybe they do, and they choose not to.

Rayne builds a receiver. It’s bare bones, wired from scraps and memory. It doesn’t do much, just hums when he’s near, spitting static. Sometimes, he thinks the static shifts. Sometimes it’s silent.

He leaves it on anyway.

Just in case.

Another month. Maybe three.

Time is measured in power loops now. In how many cycles a file can run before memory resets.

AUTOSAVE_011.

AUTOSAVE_012.

AUTOSAVE_013.

The songs lose their shape, their melodies collapsing. What’s left is beautiful, broken noise. Code threaded through rhythm, distortion wrapped around something that once meant harmony.

He dreams sometimes. Glitched dreams.

—A street—

—A station—

—A song sung in two voices—

—Rain hitting metal—

—Someone calling him by a name he doesn’t remember—

He wakes shaking.

He doesn’t record those.

Eventually, he finds a place. A hollowed-out space between two signal boosters, shielded and quiet. Nothing transmits here. Not by accident, he thinks. Someone long before him made it that way.

It isn’t sleep. Not really. But there’s a failsafe in his systems, deep-coded, rarely used. Something meant to preserve energy in hostile environments. A command he can trigger manually, if only once. Shutdown, but with memory intact. A sleep without dreams. A silence without death.

He rewires the override himself, just enough to tether him to a heartbeat he might still one day follow. Then he lowers his systems, one by one, until the dark takes him.

It feels like a grave.

It feels like home.

He decides it’s time.

There’s a night—he doesn’t know when, doesn’t try to date it—where something changes.

He’s near the surface. Not close enough to be seen, but close enough to feel the rhythm of the city above. Traffic pulses through grid-lines. Advert-drones whisper promises through cracked speakers. Somewhere, someone is laughing—drunk, delighted, alive.

Rayne leans against the wall, eyes closed, just listening.

Then—

A song.

Not data-stitched. Not engineered. Just a voice, high and reedy and off-key. Human.

It hits like an error spike. A flash-memory too fast to parse.

He remembers a rooftop. A voice like that. Young. Cracked with joy.

He doesn’t know why it hurts.

He climbs halfway up a service stairwell just to hear more, but the voice is gone, as suddenly as it came, and only silence remains.

That night, he walks back to the hollowed-out room, every step deliberate. Measured like a countdown.

He rewires the antenna. Not to listen, but to store. Anything that drifts through, it’ll catch.

He places a palm to the back panel of his neck and activates the shutdown protocol.

The interface warns him:

EXTENDED SUSPENSION MODE MAY AFFECT MEMORY INTEGRITY.

RESUME TIMECODE: UNDEFINED.

DO YOU WISH TO CONTINUE?

He doesn’t hesitate.

CONFIRMED.

His eyes dim.

His breath stutters.

Systems collapse inward, folding like petals at night.

And just like that—

Rayne goes still.

For twenty-five years, there is nothing. No sound. No updates. No time. Just stillness, a perfect hush, buried deep beneath the world’s noise.

Until—

A flicker.

The room hums. So does Rayne.

Very softly.

Very slowly.

His systems stir. First a blink. Then a spark. Then a cascade of reboots, low-power diagnostics lighting up his dormant code. Every line of him aches from where he's been frozen inside his own skin.

WAKE FUNCTION INITIATED.

MEMORY STABILITY: 34%

SYSTEM TIME ERROR: ∞

RESTORE? Y/N

He doesn’t answer. He can’t.

But something in him says yes.

Vaguely he is aware of the city blazing above him, and in the distance—barely audible—there’s music.

Real music.

Not corporate synth-pop. Not NuYu’s demos. Raw. Strange. Angry.

It pulls something loose in his chest. He doesn’t know who made it, but it’s not manufactured, and that matters.

Weakly, as if in response, he begins to compose.

The songs don’t come easily because he isn’t fully online. His mind lags behind, slipping in and out of clarity, glitching on phrases, on feelings. There are days he replays a chord for hours because it almost reminds him of something. Of someone.

His system records them anyway.

AUTOSAVE_002

REMNANT_DRAFT03

SIGNAL_13A

Some tracks are just melody lines, hollow, wandering, as if unsure where to land. Others glide with rhythm, but no beat. They fracture halfway through. One has vocals. Only a handful of words, but he doesn’t remember writing them:

I hope you remember the good parts.

He listens on loop. It’s his voice. It has to be. But there’s something too young in it, too soft, like it was recorded before all this. Before the static settled in. He adds a harmony underneath. Glitched. Muted. Barely-there. Just enough to feel like a memory singing back.

He fades in and out of consciousness.

He waits in the dark.

PART TWO

Kairo should be asleep, but the Ghost Lines hum louder at night.

He curls beside a rusted stack of comms panels, coaxing life from a loopback receiver he scavenged three days ago. The wiring’s a mess. His fingers are bleeding from where the casing split, but the signal keeps slipping in and out like a whisper in fog, and Kairo hates unsolved puzzles more than he hates blood.

The air is heavy with solder smoke. Static crackles in the wires. He adjusts the gain again.

Nothing.

Nothing.

Nothing.

And then—

Not nothing.

A note.

Too clean to be environmental. Too warped to be programmed.

A single tone, low and melancholy. The kind of note you don’t write—you feel. The kind that clings to your ribs and vibrates there long after the air goes quiet.

Kairo freezes.

He adjusts the frequency, slowly now, like he’s afraid it’ll vanish.

Another note follows.

Then silence.

Then a broken cluster of chords, half-wrong like they’ve been stitched back together after falling apart. A breathy glitch in the loop. A reverb tail that doesn’t belong.

It’s not a broadcast. It’s not music.

It’s someone trying to remember how to make music.

“Holy shit,” Kairo whispers.

He plugs the waveform into his cortex port. It pulses behind his eyes—jagged, aching. His brain lights up in colours he’s never seen before.

The music is unstructured. No beat. Just pain and longing, buried under digital fracture.

But beneath the distortion…

A voice.

Not a full song; it’s just a fragment. A hum. There are no words, but somehow it hurts.

Kairo pulls off the headset, breath sharp. He sits in silence for a second, then rewinds the sample. Plays it again.

There! It’s faint, but now he’s sure. A glitch in the pitch curve. A delay in the resonance.

That’s not software.

That’s not a bot.

That’s a person.

The moment the track ends, it’s gone. Not paused. Not disconnected. Gone.

Kairo stares at the waveform, just dead air now, no trace, no bounceback. He swears, loud and inventive, and with such conviction that the rats skitter under the pipes. His fingers fly across the keys of his cracked tablet, rerouting the node trace.

Nothing.

The file didn’t come from the Hollow servers, and not from any known node. It was local, from somewhere nearby. Somewhere in the bones of this relay site.

“You don’t just drop sound like that and vanish,” he mutters, already rerouting power.

Wires spark. His portable rig screeches. He overloads the gain and catches a ping—tiny, low-frequency, like the tail-end of a secret trying not to be found.

Kairo’s hands shake as he isolates the frequency.

“Come on, come on, come on—”

It blips again. Further west. A non-standard waveform, buried under static like it’s been camouflaged. Whatever this is, it’s compatible.

Whatever this is, it knows how to hide.

He grabs his portable gear, crams tools into his threadbare duffel, and bolts down the corridor without checking for trip sensors.

“I’m gonna find you,” he mutters to the dark. “You don’t get to drop a heartbreak chord like that and disappear. That’s illegal. I’m emotionally compromised now.”

He skids around the corner, boots thudding through rust-flecked water.

Another flicker of tone pulses through the relay’s substructure. Barely a whisper, but it moves, shifting like it's avoiding him.

Kairo laughs—breathless, wild.

“Oh you’re good. You’re so good. But I’m better.”

His sync mod kicks into overdrive, rerouting audio filters, sniffing for anomalies in the ambient echo. It's like chasing a ghost through a haunted reverb chamber. The tones glitch between walls, dip below range, and slip through gaps no signal should fit through.

And then he skids to a stop, boots soaked with tunnel water and oil.

A new fragment pulses out. Faint. Fuzzed. But this one’s different.

There’s structure, not just glitch and mood. It’s a descending scale. Minor key. Almost like a… response.

Kairo sinks to the floor, heart hammering, breath caught halfway to panic.

“You’re alive,” he whispers. “You’re actually alive down here.”

He doesn’t send a call out to Soahn.

Not yet.

This one’s his.

Just for a moment longer.

Kairo’s not sure how long he’s gone without sleep. His eyes feel gritty, not entirely his anymore.

He’s looped the same seven-second audio fragment at least a thousand times. He’s stripped it apart, rebuilt it, inverted it, slowed it, even fed it through a glitch cascade just to see if the data would hold.

It did.

Too clean to be random. Too broken to be deliberate.

“You’re not a system echo,” he mutters, dragging the wave into a new track folder. “You’re not Hollow tech. You’re… you.”

He tags the file: S0NG_UNTITLED_A. Then a second one: S0NG_UNTITLED_B. They’re not full songs, not really. Just shivers in waveform. But they hum.

He tightens the sync band on his neck and routes the echo through his implants, just to feel it more clearly. The tone wraps around him like silk.

There’s no face. No image data. No identity signature. But he knows it’s someone.

Someone aching. Someone who remembers the sound of being loved, even if they can’t remember the person.

“I hope you remember the good parts,” Kairo murmurs aloud, quoting the last sliver of lyric embedded in Fragment F. “Where did you go?”

Soahn hasn’t noticed yet. Or if he has, he hasn’t said anything.

Kairo’s OK with that. He doesn’t want to share this yet, this too fragile, too sacred thing, like finding the glint of a star before it dies.

He isolates another frequency and feeds it through a cracked sub-vocal tuner. The melody’s incomplete, but it’s getting clearer.

Whoever this is, they’re somewhere deep.

And they’re still singing.

Soahn is concerned because Kairo isn’t speaking much, but Kairo hasn’t got time to reassure him. He hunches over his rig like it’s bleeding something precious, eyes sharp, movements clipped. The whole space hums with a jittery energy that’s not music but wants to be.

He senses Soahn watching from the doorway, tucked into shadow. Not hiding. Just giving space.

The waveform pulses on screen. Glitched. Frayed. A melody spun from quiet ruin. The sound wraps around them, strange, un-polished, but achingly human.

“That’s not one of ours,” Soahn says.

Kairo shakes his head. “Nope.” He doesn’t look away from the screen. He taps a restless rhythm against his thigh. “I didn’t build this. Didn’t mod it. Didn’t lift it from a synth bank. This just… arrived, through a dead relay port. No ID. No metadata. Just this.”

He hits play again.

The fragment shivers out.

Soahn closes his eyes. “It feels old,” he murmurs. “But not outdated.”

“Yeah,” Kairo says. “Like it’s been… waiting.”

“For what?”

“For someone.” Kairo’s voice softens. “This isn’t tech-drift. It’s a person. I can feel it.”

Soahn finally moves closer and rests a hand lightly on Kairo’s shoulder. “Then we should find them.”

Kairo turns, eyes gleaming with too many thoughts and something just shy of awe.

“We will,” he says. “I’m already tracing the line.”

Soahn nods once. As the loop plays again, Kairo’s thoughts dart sideways—brief, but insistent. Soahn mentioned someone recently. An ex-military type, quiet, kept mostly to himself down in the lower tunnels. Good in a crisis. Not much of a talker, but maybe that was the point. Kairo hadn’t followed up at the time. Too many signals, too many distractions. But now... now he’s starting to understand what building something powerful might look like.

Without a word, Soahn pulls a second headset from the wall and sits beside him.

They listen in silence. Two signals, attuned.

And somewhere, beneath the city’s skin, Rayne sleeps, unaware that someone is listening.

Signal trace active. Pattern anomaly detected.

Source: UNKNOWN NODE.

Bounce Path: FAILED.

Reverse Sync: UNSTABLE.

Status: INCOMPLETE—BUT REPEATABLE.

The terminal chirps. Kairo’s head snaps up like he’s been slapped.

“That’s it. That’s it that’s it that’s it—”

Soahn, who hasn’t moved in an hour, opens his eyes. “Did it answer?”

“No,” Kairo breathes, already typing, already splicing. “But it flinched. Something flinched.”

He pulls up the spectral map. The signal flares, dies, then blinks again. 

“Soahn—Soahn, do you see this?? It shifted its signal port. Scrambled the ID string. But the distortion curve is identical. That’s the same person. It’s a person.”

“Then they know they’ve been heard,” Soahn says quietly.

That realisation lands differently. Kairo stops. “Do you think they’re scared?” he says.

Soahn doesn’t answer.

The screen flickers again. The signal blinks on, just for a moment. A single, low, echoing note, barely long enough to catch.

And in its place: A line of text, auto-generated. Unattributed.

You’re not supposed to hear this.

Kairo goes still.

“...Too bad,” he says. “We’re listening now.”

Kairo doesn’t eat. Barely blinks. He moves like someone who’s just remembered they left the oven on in a past life and now the whole world smells like smoke.

The signal flinched. Then shifted. And now it’s drifting.

He reroutes every antenna dish on the eastside net. Taps the old tunnel spectrums. Even pulls a cracked modulator from a bin of “scrap” Soahn told him not to touch.

“You said it was fried,” Kairo mutters.

“I said it was dangerous,” Soahn replies, not looking up. “You’ve just proven both.”

A spark flies from the console. Kairo hisses and sucks his fingers.

“Worth it.”

The map on-screen redraws. A ghost-arc pings low beneath the city. A cold-zone, long since labelled deadband. It pulses once.

And then, like a heart remembering its own rhythm, it pings again.

“Ohhh yes. There you are.”

He scrapes together coordinates and pings the oldest emergency conduits. Layer after layer, his world narrows. From city-wide, to borough, to block. To tunnel. To room.

Soahn appears behind him. “You’re taking us down there, aren’t you?”

Kairo nods. “Bring a jacket. It’s gonna be weird.”

The tunnel is barely a tunnel anymore, its metal ribs infected with rot, cables like spiderwebs and the stench of old coolant bleeding into stone. No one’s been here since… ever.

“I think this was a maintenance bay,” Kairo says, voice muffled behind a breather mask. “Maybe even an old node hub. Pre-2070. Back when they still had people working on the lines down here.”

“Do we count as people working on the lines down here?” Soahn asks softly.

Kairo grins. “Some days.”

They pass faded signage. One reads: Chancery Lane. The next one used to be words, but it’s unreadable now.

They’re both tired, steps weighty, dragging a little.

Kairo stops suddenly. Soahn almost bumps into him.

“There,” Kairo whispers, pointing.

The door is more like a panel now, metal bent outward from the inside.

Soahn reaches out and touches the edge. “You sure?” he asks.

“Yeah,” Kairo says. “He’s in there.”

The door is stuck fast, warped by time and rust. Kairo wedges his shoulder against it. Soahn braces the frame. Together, they force it back inch by inch, metal shrieking like torture.

A gust of stale air spills out, cold and stagnant. Forgotten.

They shuffle into the room.

Silence. Dust. A faint, low hum.

And in the centre of it all, curled beside a long-dead interface node, head tucked against one shoulder, arms looped around his own chest like they’re the only thing he trusts—

A man.

Soahn inhales sharply. “He looks—”

“—Synthetic.” Kairo finishes. “I thought they were just stories.” His voice has gone soft. Reverent.

He kneels. Doesn’t touch.

Just says:

“Hey.”

The synth doesn’t move. His eyes are open, but unfocused. A loop caught mid-exit. Like the system paused just before goodbye.

Kairo frowns, glances back at Soahn. “He’s not fully here.”

Soahn kneels beside him and places two fingers near the synth’s jawline, just shy of contact. There’s warmth. A hint of current.

His voice drops into a hush. “He’s listening. Just not ready.”

Kairo sits cross-legged beside the synth like they’re already friends.

“Well,” he says. “We’ve got time.”

And they wait.

They wait while the system stabilises and the synth stops glitching. Then they wait while the signal falls silent again.

Kairo sets up a makeshift relay node in the corner of the room. It buzzes softly, blinking in uneven rhythm, like a heartbeat still finding its way. He doesn't speak much. Just hums under his breath as he tinkers, soldering wires with a borrowed arc spark and whispering to the machine like it can understand him.

Soahn drags in a pair of folding mats and a rusted-out lantern he somehow convinces to glow soft and low. He keeps to himself, fingers moving across a notepad like he's composing something wordless. Sometimes he pauses, glancing toward the synth, never long enough to make it a stare, just long enough to check he's still with them.

The synth doesn’t move. He’s curled where they found him, slouched in the corner like forgotten tech. No boot-up, no system check, just a flicker now and then behind his eyes, his breath too steady to be off-cycle, too shallow to be asleep.

So they wait.

They eat in silence, sharing a single thermos of black-market tea. They talk in low tones about nothing—circuit pathways, the weird way the tunnels curve near the old metro line, whether Kairo could make a synth beat from the hum of the room.

And all the while, the synth stays still. But they can sense that he’s aware, listening.

Kairo leaves a small loop running on the cracked speaker—a gentle melody, glitchy, its fragments stitched into something hopeful. He calls it “Reboot Song” and acts like it's nothing, like it just happened.

Soahn knows better, but he doesn't say a word.

Hours blur. Maybe a day. Maybe two.

And then—

A shift.

Small.

So small Soahn might’ve missed it if he hadn’t looked up at that exact moment.

A twitch of the synth’s fingers.

Then, slowly, his lips part, wide enough to shape a single word.

“…Rayne.”

Kairo yells a noise like a gasp collided with a cheer and drops the coil of wires in his hands. “OH—hey! You’re—you’re awake! You’re alive! You’re—holy shit—hi!”

Soahn doesn’t speak, just breathes out a long, steady sigh of relief.

“Rayne?! Is that what you’re called? Are you Rayne??” Kairo’s words come out too fast, all in one bit gulp of shimmering happiness.

Rayne blinks. His voice comes again, halting and dry, like it’s been in sleep mode for years.

“You stayed.”

Kairo crouches beside him instantly, almost vibrating with joy. “Course we did. What, you think we’d just leave you down here, sleeping like some sad synth prince in a broken fairytale?”

Rayne manages something close to a smile. It’s strange. Glitch-soft. But real.

“I thought… I was alone.”

Soahn moves closer, settling on the edge of the mat beside him. “You’re not alone.” No more than that. Just the truth.

His words linger in the stillness where loneliness once lived.

There’s something new there, now, in the quiet.

A note of beginning.
