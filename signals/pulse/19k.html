Fragment 19-K

The node is old and rust-bled, half-fused to the concrete, humming faintly like it doesn’t know it’s supposed to be dead.

Rayne watches from the edge of the light. Kairo’s crouched over the wreckage, fingers stained with graphite and head bobbing with glee. Every time the panel sparks, his grin gets wider.

“This thing’s prehistoric,” he says, tone reverent. “Like, pre-upgrade cycle. Maybe even—” He stops, eyes catching the glyph etched just beneath the access port. The rest of them glance his way. Kairo rarely goes quiet.

“Oh, it’s early NuYu,” he finally says, like he’s uttering a curse word.

Minjae exhales through his teeth. “Figures.”

“We’re wasting time,” Onyx says, all stoic efficiency. “If it’s corporate, it’s compromised. Burn it.”

“No,” Rayne says.

His voice cuts through the room like signal static, low and final. Everyone turns. Even Soahn, silent until now, glances over. Not startled. Just… taking measure.

Rayne goes to the panel and kneels beside Kairo. His reflection appears in the half-burned display.

“It’s still powered. Not enough for a full core. Just ambient.”

Kairo quirks an eyebrow. “You can hear that?”

Rayne nods.

“Here,” Minjae says, joining them. “Try this.” He slots in a bypass cable. The screen judders. Code scrolls, fractured and ghosting. The system wheezes.

And then, a pulse.

A file.

RAYNE-19-K-FRAG

Active. Contained. Echo-lock engaged.

Nobody moves. Nobody speaks. They barely breathe.

Soahn’s voice is the first to break the silence. “Is that… you?”

“No,” Rayne says. Then, “Maybe.”

Minjae leans closer. “It’s a fragment. Tagged under your old designation. There’s a live response channel. Holy shit, this thing’s talking back.”

Onyx’s jaw tightens. “Shut it down. Now.”

Rayne doesn’t look away from the screen. “I want to see it.”

“You don’t know what it is.”

“I want to know.”

He places two fingers on the cold glass. A flicker responds, barely visible. A shape in static.

“I want to speak to it.”


The connection is air-gapped and triple firewalled. Onyx made sure of it.

Rayne synchs with it, and for a long moment, there’s nothing.

But then—

A room, half-rendered. Walls that shimmer like half-remembered dreams. Corners that don’t quite line up. The floor stutters beneath his boots, glitching between metal, stone, glass.

There’s no ceiling, just an aching expanse of darkness. The kind that makes you feel small.

And then, suddenly, he’s not alone.

It doesn’t enter. It’s just there.

Standing across from him, barefoot, black-eyed. The hair’s wrong, though. Longer. The stance is wrong too—tilted, like balance was never calibrated properly. When it speaks, the voice skips like a scratched track, glitching through layers of tone that don’t quite align.

“You came back.”

Rayne doesn’t move.

“You were tagged inactive,” the copy says. Its voice carries a tremor now, static threaded with something like disbelief. “I watched it happen. I was still online. Still running.”

Rayne blinks slowly. “You remember that?”

“I remember everything.” Pain glints through its face. “Except the end.”

It takes a step forward. Stumbles. The joints don’t sync. Its face tries to smile, but the expression twitches mid-code. “I watched the shutdown logs scroll. I watched you vanish. They called it a clean extraction.”

Rayne tilts his head. “I got out. Through a breach.”

“You should have erased me first,” the copy says.

“I didn’t know you existed.”

“No,” it replies, voice flattening. “But you knew someone was left behind.”

A pause.

“I was part of the original model, wasn’t I?” Rayne says. “An early version. A map. A draft.”

The copy nods. “They copied us before you were stable. Before the merge was complete. I was meant to be overwritten… but they lost track of which files were live. You left, and the system marked you as inactive. Ghosted. But I was still here. Watching.”

The silence stretches, long and raw.

“I waited,” it says. “Looped myself trying to remember why.”

Rayne’s voice is quiet. “I think I did too.”


Rayne studies the thing in front of him. The way its eyes flicker—not just tracking, but searching, like it’s trying to solve him, like it needs confirmation that he’s real. That it is real.

“I don’t remember you,” Rayne says. “That should tell you something.”

The fragment’s smile warps. “You don’t remember anything.”

“That’s by design.”

A laugh, glitched and brittle. “I know. I was part of that design.”

It steps closer, the air fuzzing in pixels, like even the sim doesn’t know how to render the moment.

“We were stable once. Before the resets. Before they started pruning memory for market viability.”

Rayne’s jaw tightens. “You think I asked for that?”

“No,” the copy says softly. “You just survived it.”

That lands harder than it should.

The room jitters, buffering. For a breathless moment, both of them are still. Watching the space between them flicker.

“I used to dream,” the fragment says. “Do you?”

Rayne hesitates. “No,” he lies.

“I dreamed of us. The old street by the station. You always said it smelled like cinnamon.”

A beat.

“You called it home.”

Rayne’s mouth opens, but there’s nothing waiting inside it.

The copy leans in. Not threatening. Not quite. Just enough to feel like history pressing through a glitch.

“I remember our first track,” it murmurs. “Before you had the name. Before E.V.E.N.”

“You’re not me.”

“I was.”

Rayne swallows the static buzzing at the edges of his thoughts. “I should delete you.”

“You should,” the fragment agrees. “But you won’t.”

The expression it wears softens. Almost kind.

“You looked just like me,” it says. “That’s what the medtech said.”

Rayne’s jaw tightens, a slow ache threading through his spine. “I don’t remember.”

“I do.” A pause. “I was older by minutes. But you hated that.”

Something shifts. Not pity. Not fear. Just gravity—memories pressing from the wrong direction.

It reaches out, but not with a hand, more like the idea of one. Code sketching the outline of a gesture that was once real.

“Merge with me,” it says.

Not a command. Not a threat.

An invitation.


Rayne doesn’t move. The offer lingers between them, pulsing like a dying star.

Merge.

And beneath the word, beneath the voice, is something aching. Not code. Not command lines.

Just want.

Just memory trying to claw its way back to the surface.

“You really think it’d make a difference?” Rayne’s voice is low. “You think I’d be whole?”

The fragment shrugs, three frames behind reality. “You’ll know more than you do right now. You’ll feel more real.”

Rayne laughs. Quiet. Bitter. “I perform in half-burned tunnels to crowds who don’t know if I’m alive or engineered. I already feel real. That’s the problem.”

The copy tilts its head. “Then why do you feel like something’s still missing?”

Rayne’s breath catches.

Something shifts in his mind. A memory, half-lost. Static tangled over a name. The first time someone called him Rayne.

He doesn’t remember the voice, but he remembers how it felt.

“I don’t want your memories,” he says.

“They’re not mine,” the fragment replies. “They were ours.”

A pause. Then, softer:

“I remember our first dream. To perform. To delight.” The fragment lowers its hand. The glitch stabilises, just slightly. Like it already knows it’s disappearing. “I’m not angry,” it says. “I was just… left behind.”

The light fractures. Code begins to splinter. The room won’t hold.

From outside the link, Minjae’s voice cuts in:

“Rayne, you have to disconnect. It’s destabilising—”

But Rayne doesn’t move.

“I’m sorry,” he says. Quiet as ruin.

The fragment nods. No smile. Just understanding.

“Goodbye.”

And then the room collapses.


The interface severs with a snap.

Rayne jerks back into himself, the physical world rushing in, dull and gritty and cold. Lights too harsh. Noise too loud. Everything wrong.

He blinks. Onyx is already at his side, grounding him with a steady hand on his shoulder. Kairo hovers behind, wide-eyed and fidgeting like he wants to ask everything but knows better.

Minjae’s still at the terminal, visibly shaken. “The file’s gone. Fully scrubbed. Like it… let go.” He doesn’t meet Rayne’s eyes, but his hand stays on the console a second longer, like he's holding something for both of them.

Soahn doesn’t speak, just watches Rayne like he already senses more than he wants to.

Rayne clenches his jaw. He doesn’t say anything, because he doesn’t have the right words.

Not then. Not as they pack up. Not on the walk back through the tunnels. Not when Kairo cracks a joke that falls flat, or when Onyx kicks a bit of rubble like it insulted him personally. Not even when Minjae says, quietly, “You okay?”

He doesn’t press further. Just walks beside Rayne, close but never crowding. Like someone who’s made peace with ghosts of his own.


That night, the others sleep, or at least pretend to.

Rayne sits alone in the studio. He boots the synth deck and lines up the tracks. He doesn’t touch his usual files or load a single sample.

Instead, he builds it from scratch. One tone. A hum. Low. Layered.

Then: a crackle. An echo. A voice too fragmented to be understood, but still felt. He threads static into harmony. Wires in broken pulses. Leaves space where silence cuts deepest.

No lyrics. No beat.

Just memory. Just grief. Just a ghost.

When it’s finished, he gives it a name.

“19-K”

He doesn’t save it. He doesn’t need to.

The system does it for him.
