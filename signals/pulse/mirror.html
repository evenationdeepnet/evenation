The Glitch in the Mirror

It started with a flicker that wasn’t light or sound, but feeling.

Soahn sat in the far corner of the bunker, eyes closed, trying to catalogue the echo. It brushed past his consciousness like static—emotional noise with no clear source.

He ran a systems check through his neural AI bond. No spike in proximity alerts. No broadcast frequencies nearby. The guys were all accounted for: Kairo was rewiring his soundboard with half a burrito in one hand; Rayne sat in stillness and silence, processing whatever took place within him; Onyx dozed on a nearby bunk, one arm slung lazily over his face; and Minjae was draped across a mattress on the floor, muttering about rhyme schemes and bad synthpunk lyrics.

No one was upset. Not visibly, anyway. Not enough to explain the feeling clawing down Soahn’s spine like a memory playing on repeat.


It happened again the next night, but this time that strange feeling came stronger.

A wave of emotion that wasn’t his own, and wasn’t one of the others’. Grief, maybe. Loneliness twisted so tight it bordered on nausea. Not language—not a voice. Just the sense of being unmade.

Soahn couldn’t fall asleep, not with that constant humming wave. Neural-linked dreams were mostly memory consolidation. But even when he did rest, he felt it there too.

That presence. A pulse of sorrow so deep it didn’t even feel human anymore.


The mirror found him on the third night.

He was wandering. He did that when the emotions built too high, slipping away while the others slept or sparked, letting the quiet drag his thoughts into order.

The corridor beyond the living area curved sharply, a jagged offshoot of the old Ghost Line access tunnels. Most of the doors down here were sealed, rusted or scorched from the early purges. But one had cracked open a few centimetres. Enough for cold air to drift through. Enough to feel like something was waiting.

Soahn cautiously pushed it open.

The room inside was empty.

Well… kind of.

The walls were scraped bare, scratched deep like something had fought to remember itself. There were faded tracks on the floor, indicating that someone had slept here once, long ago. And propped in the corner, nearly buried behind old emergency rations and a metal frame, was a tall, cracked mirror.

Its surface shimmered faintly, even in the dark.

Soahn started toward it, but stopped.

The reflection didn’t match his movements.

He blinked and raised his hand.

The version in the mirror raised it too, but a beat too slow. Almost trembling.

A flicker crossed the glass. Distortion? Data? No, it was more like… emotion.

Soahn’s own face looked back at him, but it was fractured, flickering at the edges. The shadows curved wrong around his cheekbones. His mouth opened in the reflection, though he hadn’t spoken.

And for one frozen second, the mirrored version looked like it was crying out.

Soahn staggered back. The connection immediately broke and the mirror flickered, once, then stilled.

Just his reflection again.

Nothing strange.

Nothing wrong.


By the time he got back to the bunker, Soahn had already decided not to tell the others. Mainly because they wound’t believe him. Partially because he didn’t believe it himself.

Kairo offered him a leftover protein bar.

Minjae cracked a joke about sleep deprivation.

Onyx scanned him with a slow side-eye and said, “You look like you saw your own ghost.”

Rayne didn’t look at him, just said quietly: “Some echoes aren't meant to be decrypted.”

Soahn swallowed and offered them the glimmer of a nonchalant smile.

But that night, he dreamed of the mirror, and this time, the version of him inside it whispered something he couldn’t quite understand. His own voice, but not, like someone had recorded him grieving.

Like someone had preserved it.


The dream came back, night after night, so vivid, so real.

Soahn stood in the mirror room, barefoot, wrapped in silence. The room didn’t have any doors, windows or walls, just dark glass that stretched out in every direction, endless as thought.

He raised his hand again.

The reflection didn’t follow.

It stared back, lips parted, eyes red-rimmed like it had wept something ancient dry. When it finally spoke, it wasn’t with words, but with the weight of sorrow pressed into bone, coded into blood. A feeling that had no shape except loss.

Soahn stumbled forward, reaching for the glass, feeling like his head might explode from the pressure of it.

But the moment his fingers touched it—

Pain. 

Not physical, more like emotional static scraping through his chest, burning where his mind joined his machine.


He woke with a shout, struggling within his blankets, breath ragged, systems spiking.

Then there was movement—someone beside him.

Minjae. He crouched beside Soahn, arms resting on bent knees, worry pinched at the corners of his mouth. “You glitched,” he said quietly. “Like... dream overload. Twitching like a dying synthpop DJ.”

Soahn closed his eyes. “Sorry.”

Minjae shrugged. “Don’t be. I only watch because you talk in your sleep.”

“Really?” Soahn’s heart stuttered. “What did I say?”

Minjae tilted his head. “Something about a mirror. A reflection.”

A beat.

“Your expression…” He looked away. “I didn’t like seeing that. Whatever you dreamed… it had to be real, right?”

“I don’t know.” Soahn pushed himself up and crossed his legs, lowering his head. It felt heavy. “I think I accessed something I wasn’t meant to.”

“From what? The creepy mirror?” Minjae said it like a joke, but his voice wasn’t mocking.

Soahn nodded.

Minjae sighed. “You should’ve said something.”

“I didn’t know what it was.”


Later that day, the others found them both sitting near the broken data node at the back of the hideout. Soahn was reading a book. Minjae was doodling something unhinged on the wall—some kind of screaming flower with wings.

Kairo noticed the tension first. “Did you two fuse brains and forget to tell us?”

Minjae didn’t look up. “Soahn’s haunted.”

“By what?” Rayne asked, as if it was a totally normal thing.

Soahn raised his eyes from the book. “I think it’s… a version of me that remembers things I’ve never lived.”

“That doesn’t make any sense,” Onyx said.

“There’s this mirror,” Soahn continued, voice low. “In the tunnels. I thought it was just a dream, but I know it’s there. It shows a fractured version. Looks like me, but wrong. It’s hurting.”

Kairo frowned. “Sounds like a ghost interface. Old NuYu tech used emotional memory as biometric ID for deep encryption.”

“I’m not encrypted,” Soahn whispered. “I’m... linked.”

“Which makes you a better conduit than any of us,” Rayne said softly. “Sometimes the lines don’t know the difference between living and remembering.”

Silence.

Minjae broke it. “I say we smash the mirror.”

Onyx gave him a look. “Because that always ends well.”

“No, seriously. Every weird reflection story ends with someone not smashing it. I vote we break the cycle. Literally.”

Kairo scratched his jaw. “Or we use it.”

Rayne’s eyes were on Soahn. “Or we listen.”

Soahn looked down at his hands, resting gently on the pages of the book. They didn’t shake. But they felt like they should.


That night, the others sat with Soahn, none of them sleeping, not close enough to crowd him, but near enough that if he fell asleep and dreamed again, he was still connected to something real.

Kairo hummed a half-formed song under his breath. It sounded like something Minjae would write.

Rayne stayed near the vent, shadows painting across his face in slow pulses. At one point, barely above a whisper, he said, “I once saw someone in the mirror who wasn’t me.”

Soahn looked at him. Rayne didn’t look back. Just kept watching the dark, like he knew what it could hold.

Onyx patched a crack in the wall. Quietly. Thoughtfully. One eye always watching Soahn from the side.

And Minjae leaned back and said, “If your ghost starts rapping, at least record it. I’ll build a beat.”

Soahn smiled, small and tired. “Deal.”


The mirror remained, but it didn’t glitch again.

The reflection still looked almost like him. Just enough to ache.

Soahn tried not to stare into it for too long.

But when he did, someone always sat nearby, quietly waiting for him to wake.
