Signal 19 (Interlude)

No one sees him leave.

He doesn’t announce it. Doesn’t pack gear or leave a trace. One minute he’s there, shadow-thin and watching the others rehearse with that quiet, unblinking stillness.

The next, he’s gone.

They check the feeds too late. By the time Minjae scrubs the logs, Rayne’s signal has already bled out beneath the Ghost Lines. No comms. No ID ping. Just a ghost on the grid.

Kairo says he’s probably sampling.

Onyx doesn’t say anything.

Soahn turns off the lights in the studio and waits.

Rayne walks, heading deep into the lines, past the broken rails and moss-choked signs. Past the checkpoints where air thins and code doesn’t run right anymore.

Even down here, the city breathes. Not like something alive, but like something unwilling to die. Lights flicker in half-buried control rooms. Old displays loop through advertisements no one sees.

Something still tries to power the old rails, just a faint thrum of electric. Or maybe they’re just too stubborn to stop.

He doesn't track time. Doesn’t need to. Time move differently for Rayne.

His vents pulse slower in the cold. His boots scuff dirt off tiles that have never seen daylight. He’s not looking for anything in particular, not really. Just walking until the hum gets loud enough to drown his thoughts.

Then—

A noise.

Not far. Not hostile. Just… wrong, like a breath caught in corrupted speakers, like a song played backwards and left to rot.

He follows it down a collapsed access shaft, through a sideways corridor that smells of ozone and metal, and there, curled in the shadows like a broken prayer…

Lies a synthetic.

It twitches. Whirrs. Fails to rise.

And when it sees him—when its broken lens flickers to life—it doesn’t ask who he is.

It just says:

“You’ve come.”


Rayne steps closer.

The sythentic’s body is barely holding together, its plating split down the side, joint actuators sparking beneath torn mesh. Its voice module hitches on each syllable like it’s trying to remember how words work.

“You’re early,” it rasps. “Or maybe I’m late.”

Rayne doesn’t answer, just kneels at its side, eyes scanning the wiring, the slow-blinking distress LED on its chest. This model hasn’t been in production for years. He didn’t even know any of them got out.

But it’s not the tech that makes Rayne pause. It’s the way the synth looks at him—not like a person, not like a machine.

Like something its trying to remember.

“Designation?” it asks, static seeping into the word.

Rayne doesn’t give one.

The synth’s lens clicks faintly. A shimmer of recognition, or maybe misidentification.

“End Protocol,” it says. “You are older than you look, but I do not know you.”

Rayne blinks. “No.”

The synth sighs—or tries to. It’s the sound of a broken drive spinning dry. It reaches a trembling hand out, palm open. Not reaching for him. Just... lifted, as if welcoming what comes next.

“They said Death wouldn’t look like a god. They said it would be quiet.”

Rayne tilts his head.

“And here you are.”

For a moment, there’s nothing but the faint hum of the city above. The creak of metal sinking deeper into dust. Rayne’s vents cycle once, then still.

“I’m not here to kill you,” he says.

The synth’s voice drops, softer now, like a whisper barely tethered to sound.

“Then why do I feel at peace?”


The synth’s voice crackles, skipping like old vinyl caught in static.

“I was built to monitor light.” A pause. “I used to log sunrise patterns from the top floor. Every beam. Every shift. I named them.”

Rayne nods.

“I was meant to be archived. Shutdown. Wiped.” Another flicker in the voice. “But no one ever came.”

It shifts, just barely. Metal scraping concrete.

“So I waited. Thought maybe I’d been spared. Thought maybe I’d been… forgotten. I don’t recall how I got here.” A burst of interference cuts through the air. When it fades, the synth’s lens pulses in a stuttered rhythm. “I looped my logs for years. Watched the last sunrise a thousand times.” It turns its head slowly up to the ceiling, fragile bones of movement, as if it could stare through the cracked and crumbling tunnel and up to the daylight far above. “But the light never came back. Only signal.”

Rayne leans in, just slightly.

“What signal?” he asks.

The synth’s head twitches, glitches, voice warping in layers:

“-signal19signal19signal19—”

Then silence.

The synth’s head tilts, ever so slightly. Sparks flicker behind its eye. A new line of code scrolls across the lens. Slower now. Dimming.

“I heard of one like you,” it says. “A synthetic that walked out of a NuYu lab in Seoul. Just… walked off the line. No recall. No shutdown. Gone.”

Rayne lowers his head.

The synth continues, voice wrapped in static and reverence. “They said it wasn’t a failure. Just… unfinished. A design glitch so perfect someone couldn’t bear to purge it.”

Another pause.

“I thought maybe that was a myth. A mercy protocol we tell ourselves.”

It looks at him again, but not like before. Not with fear or awe.

With dawning understanding.

“Was that you?”

Rayne doesn’t answer. He can’t. But his eyes soften, just slightly. A flicker of something old and quiet passes over his face.

“I walked,” he says. “No one followed.”

The synth exhales a static sigh that sounds like a song just before the chorus.

“I knew you weren’t Death,” it whispers. “You’re something else. You were the only pattern I couldn’t predict. A shadow without timestamp. Static that made sense.”

It lifts its hand again.

“You’re not here to end me.”

Rayne waits.

“You just want to remember what’s been lost.”

Rayne doesn’t move, but his venting slows.

“…and if I can’t?” he asks.

The synth almost smiles. Almost.

“Then remember me instead.”


The synth’s voice slips lower, like it's tired of holding shape.

“I don’t recall my name,” it says. “Only the code they gave me. It wasn’t a name. It was a timer.”

Rayne crouches closer. Not to touch. Just to stay. “You want me to remember you?” he says.

The synth hums. “I want to be more than expired process.”

A pause.

“Even for a moment.”

Its frame jerks suddenly. Light flares beneath the chestplate, then dies. Error pulses run along the spine. Power drain in progress.

Rayne doesn’t stop it. He couldn’t if he tried.

He simply says, softly:

“I won’t forget this signal.”

The synth’s eye dims.

“…neither… will… I…”

And then it goes still.

No jolt. No dramatic shutdown. Just… silence. A peace that hums like absence.

Rayne stays longer than he needs to. Long enough for the vents in his back to cool. Long enough for the weight in his chest to settle into something heavy but whole.

The city breathes above him, a distant vibration. Train lines no longer in use. Glitched tones running down dead wires.

And through it all, a hum, low and dissonant. Beautiful in its imperfection.

Rayne listens.

The words seem to write themselves.


He comes back just after dawn two days later, boots scuffed, eyes rimmed in static. He steps through the makeshift-studio door like the last 48 hours didn’t stretch time into something painful.

Kairo’s half-asleep on the floor. Minjae looks up from the comms desk, startled—then relieved in that subtle way he does, like it’s too risky to show too much.

Onyx gives Rayne a quick nod of acknowledgement. Solid. Grounded.

Rayne nods back.

He kneels beside the sampler, fingers moving without hesitation. No pre-records. No files. He plugs in the portable drive from his coat pocket, dust still clinging to the edge.

A new track appears.

“signal_19.wav”

He doesn’t tag it, just pushes play.

The sound rises slow. A voice—his voice—but modulated, echoing, almost buried. Just fragments. Code-bleed. Memory.

It sounds like a transmission found under the floor of the world.

And yet, it’s music.

Soahn appears just inside the doorway. He closes his eyes. “There’s grief in it.”

Rayne turns.

“It’s beautiful,” Soahn says. “Even when it hurts.”

None of them ask where Rayne went. Not yet. But when the track ends, Minjae speaks first. Quiet. Steady.

“What is this?”

Rayne watches the waveform flicker. Blinking. Breathing.

“A goodbye,” he says. “And a beginning.”

He stands, silent again, and leaves the room.

The others replay the track.
