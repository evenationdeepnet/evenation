Static Bloom

The others were sleeping. Even in The Ghost Lines, downtime meant something. Rest. Cool darkness. The slow reset of systems and nerves alike.

But Rayne didn’t sleep.

The makeshift studio hummed with a low, fractured loop. Three broken audio decks connected by a tangled pulse of rewired cables. It was messy. Glitchy. Beautiful.

Rayne stood in the doorway like a sentinel, always alert for movement in the tunnels beyond.

Kairo had fallen asleep mid-mix, half-sprawled across the floor, one hand still resting on a cracked touchscreen. His breathing was shallow and rapid, his cortical link still shimmering faintly blue. Active.

The audio loop he’d been working on repeated.

And then it morphed.

Rayne tilted his head.

New sound spilled through the speakers—not music exactly, not yet. Just fragments. Strings of syllables. Data-glitches that sounded like speech but weren’t meant to be decoded.

Except Rayne could. Of course he could.

He stepped closer, silent as static.

Kairo murmured something—half-gasped—and his hand twitched. The loop changed again. Layered. Warped.

Rayne’s internal translator parsed the audio feed.

These weren’t code strings. They were lyrics. 

Dream-lyrics. Spoken mid-sync.

“Black sky… grey bloom...
…colour runs through me like noise…”

Kairo, even unconscious, was still composing.

Rayne knelt beside him.

The floor was scattered with loose nodes and copper coil. A single neon line blinked across the interface. Blue-orange. Pulsing to Kairo’s breathing.

Rayne reached out slowly and tapped the playback deck. The recording began.

“My heart’s a loopback signal
Wired in static bloom
If I glitch here…
You’ll hear me
In the noise.”

The words were raw. Rayne silently adjusted the gain, fed it through a soft layer of delay. Let it run again. And again.

A song began to form. Not fully. Not yet.

But the bones were there.


Eventually Kairo stirred, blinking awake, confused and groggy.

“Did I…” he paused, squinting. “Was I talking?”

Rayne didn’t look up from the mix. “Yes.”

Brushing dust off his cheek, Kairo sat up. “I was dreaming in waveform again, wasn’t I?” He rubbed the back of his neck, fingers brushing over the neural threads there—absent, automatic. “It’s been happening more. Like the music’s running without me.”

“You said something about blooming static.” Rayne didn’t know why the phrase made his chest tighten, like it belonged to something he hadn’t written but still missed.

Kairo yawned hugely, and then grinned lopsided. “That sounds like me.”

Rayne replayed the loop. “Do you remember it?”

“Nope.”

Rayne added a quiet filter to the vocal track. A low shimmer beneath the words.

“You’re building it,” Kairo said, watching him. Not a question.

Rayne finally looked up. “Yes. It’s beautiful.”

Kairo let the silence settle, then reached for the nearest interface. “Let’s finish it, then. Before it fades.”
